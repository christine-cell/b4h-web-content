Before a formal assessment ever takes place, every prospective client goes through an initial intake process. This is your first conversation — and your first opportunity to start building trust while gathering the information you need to place someone correctly. This lesson walks through why the intake form matters, what it covers, and how to use the official B4H form.

What you'll learn

  • Understand why the prospective client intake form is completed before the formal assessment.
  • Recognise the core areas every intake covers — contact details, diagnosis history, mobility, fall risk, and current activity level.
  • Use mobility and fall-history questions to guide safe, appropriate class placement.
  • Know when to use the official B4H form and what's required if you design your own.

Section 01

Purpose of the Intake Form

The prospective client intake form is completed before the formal assessment. It gives you essential background on the person's diagnosis, mobility, fall history, and current activity level — so you arrive at the assessment already informed, not starting from zero.

Think of this as your pre-assessment snapshot. It helps you determine which assessment tests are appropriate, what red flags to watch for, and which class might be the right fit — before you ever see them move.

Coach's Corner

Intake is more than data collection — it's your first conversation with the person. Use it to start building trust. A warm, unhurried first contact tells someone living with Parkinson's that they're in the right place.

Section 02

What the Form Covers

The intake gathers background and a simple mobility picture. Each item below tells you something you'll use when planning the assessment and choosing a class.

  1. Contact information

    Name, phone, and email. Essential for follow-up and ongoing communication.

  2. How they heard about us

    Flyer, Facebook ad, Google, or referral. Useful for understanding your marketing reach.

  3. Diagnosis date and age

    How long they've been living with Parkinson's and where they are in life gives important context.

  4. Neurologist's name

    Important for any future communication with their healthcare team if needed.

  5. Can they walk around the block independently?

    A simple but powerful mobility baseline question.

  6. Do they use a mobility aid?

    Cane, walker, or other assistive device. This affects class placement significantly.

  7. Falls in the past year?

    One of the most important fall-risk indicators. Any falls require extra attention during assessment.

  8. Endurance and stamina

    Helps set realistic expectations for their first few classes and guides intensity planning.

  9. Current activity level and exercise habits

    Are they sedentary? Already active? This shapes how you approach their first session.

Safety

Fall history is one of your most important early signals. If a prospective client reports any falls in the past year, flag it now so you give it extra attention during the formal assessment and class placement.

Section 03

Using the B4H Form

The mobility questions — walking around the block, mobility aids, falls, endurance, and current activity — exist specifically to help determine the best class for each person. You can use the official B4H prospective client intake form, or design your own.

Good to know

If you create a custom version, it must be reviewed and authorized by B4H head office before use. Your form should cover all the same core areas — contact info, diagnosis history, mobility, fall risk, and current activity level.

Key takeaways

  • The intake form is completed before the formal assessment so you arrive already informed — it's a pre-assessment snapshot, not a formality.
  • Every intake covers the same core areas: contact details, how they heard about you, diagnosis history, neurologist, mobility, fall risk, endurance, and current activity level.
  • The mobility questions exist to guide safe class placement — walking independence, mobility aids, falls, stamina, and current activity all shape where someone fits.
  • Any fall in the past year is a key risk indicator that warrants extra attention during assessment.
  • Use the official B4H form, or a custom form that covers all the same areas — but any custom version must be authorized by B4H head office first.
  • Treat intake as your first trust-building conversation, not just data collection.
