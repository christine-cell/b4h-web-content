A few simple tools make running a class smoother — a visual timer the clients can see, and music to keep the energy and rhythm up. Here's what we use, so you can play around with the options and find what works best for you.

What you'll learn

  • Which timer apps we use and why a shared visual helps clients
  • How we bring music into class to motivate clients and support rhythm and timing
  • How we build playlists around what clients want to hear

Timers & Visuals

We use a timer app in class and project it to a TV screen so everyone can follow along. You can also run it from an iPad — we find that having a visual for the clients can be beneficial. When we teach a group functional or boxing portion, we use HIIT timers to keep the intervals on track. Play around with the options and see what works best for you.

Timer app on a TV screen

We use a timer app in class and project it to a TV screen. You can also use an iPad — having a visual for the clients can be beneficial.

HIIT timers

We use HIIT timers when we teach a group functional or boxing portion to keep the work and rest intervals on track.

Boxing iTimer Free app

A free boxing timer app we use to run class intervals.

Music

We use Spotify, and you can check out our playlists at Boxing 4 Health. Music plays an important part in a class — it helps motivate the clients, and it helps with their rhythm and timing as well. We also ask clients what they want to listen to and build playlists based on that too. Typically it's a mix of rock classics from the 60's, 70's and 80's.

Spotify playlists

Client-driven playlists

Coach's Corner

Sometimes breaking out into dance during a class is a great way to have some fun and get the clients to loosen up too.

Key takeaways

  • Project a timer app to a TV or run it from an iPad so clients have a shared visual.
  • Use HIIT timers for the group functional and boxing portions; Boxing iTimer is a free option.
  • Music motivates clients and supports rhythm and timing — build playlists around what they want to hear.
