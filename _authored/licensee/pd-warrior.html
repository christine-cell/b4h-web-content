Section 01

What Is PD Warrior?

PD Warrior is an exercise-based Parkinson's treatment program founded in 2011 by two specialist neurological physiotherapists in Australia. It was built from the ground up on the principles of neuroplasticity — specifically designed to retrain the brain and improve Parkinson's symptoms through high-intensity, neuroactive exercise.

The program's founder, Melissa McConaghy, is a specialist neurological physiotherapist with a Master's in Health Science in Neurological Physiotherapy. She was awarded Australian Physiotherapist of the Year in 2019 and has spent over 20 years working with people with Parkinson's. PD Warrior has since grown into the world's largest online Parkinson's exercise community.

A published pilot study by Tucak et al. (2024, Internal Medicine Journal) provided the first peer-reviewed evidence that the PD Warrior 10-Week Challenge program significantly improves motor outcomes, mobility, daily living, and quality of life in people with early Parkinson's.

Section 02

The Science Behind It

Did you know that your brain has the ability to change and adapt throughout a person's entire life? This is called neuroplasticity — and once you start teaching our program to seniors and people with Parkinson's, you will see these changes happening in real time.

Think of it as the brain's way of rewiring itself in response to experiences, learning, and challenges. Just like a muscle gets stronger when you exercise it, the brain becomes more flexible and efficient when it's challenged through new activities and movement.

When you engage in physical activity, the brain isn't sitting idle. Exercise stimulates the brain and encourages it to create new connections between nerve cells (neurons). These connections allow different parts of the brain to communicate better — improving memory, attention, coordination, and problem-solving.

Regular exercise also increases blood flow to the brain, delivering more oxygen and nutrients. It triggers the release of neurotransmitters — dopamine, serotonin, endorphins — that help with mood, stress, and mental wellbeing. Staying physically active doesn't just benefit the body. It actively maintains and enhances neuroplasticity, keeping the brain sharp and adaptable throughout life.

Groundbreaking 2024 research by de Laat et al. found that high-intensity exercise may actually increase dopamine transporter availability and neuromelanin levels in the substantia nigra — the exact brain region Parkinson's destroys. This suggests exercise isn't just managing symptoms — it may be slowing the disease itself.

Sources: de Laat et al. (2024) · Tucak et al., Internal Medicine Journal (2024) · McConaghy, PD Warrior · Hirsch et al., Parkinsonism & Related Disorders (2016)

New Neural Pathways

Exercise stimulates neurons to form new connections — improving how different brain regions communicate. PD Warrior exercises are specifically designed to maximize this neuroplastic response.

High-Intensity = More Benefit

PD Warrior research confirms that high-intensity exercise produces greater neuroplastic changes than low-intensity exercise. This is why both PD Warrior and B4H emphasize pushing hard — it's clinically purposeful.

Dopamine Support

2024 research shows high-intensity exercise may increase dopamine transporter availability in the substantia nigra — potentially protecting the very neurons Parkinson's is destroying.

Proven Outcomes

Tucak et al. (2024) found PD Warrior participants showed significant improvements in motor function, balance, mobility, daily living activities, and quality of life following the 10-Week Challenge program.

BDNF Production

Exercise increases Brain-Derived Neurotrophic Factor — a protein that supports neuron survival, growth, and new neural connection formation. PD Warrior's intensity is designed to maximize BDNF output.

Community Effect

PD Warrior's research highlights that group exercise classes increase adherence and satisfaction — the social motivation compounds the physical benefit. Sound familiar? That's B4H too.

Section 03

The B4H Requirement

Good to know

2 PD Warrior exercises per class — required. At Boxing 4 Health, every class design must include a minimum of 2 PD Warrior exercises. This is not optional — it's a core part of our program design and ensures every class delivers the neuroactive, Parkinson's-specific stimulus that our clients need. You will review the PD Warrior exercise library and then demonstrate your understanding to one of our head instructors before you teach.

Here's exactly how to meet this requirement:

  1. Review the PD Warrior Exercise Library

    Study the PD Warrior exercises provided in your B4H training resources. Understand what each exercise targets — which symptom it addresses, which movement principle it applies (amplitude, rhythm, balance, dual-task), and how to modify it for different ability levels.

  2. Practice the Exercises Yourself

    You can't teach what you can't demonstrate. Learn each PD Warrior exercise from the inside out — feel what it demands, notice where clients might struggle, and think about how you'd cue it using the VIGOR framework. Practice until you can do it confidently without looking at notes.

  3. Demonstrate to a Head Instructor

    Book a session with one of our B4H head instructors to demonstrate your exercises. They'll confirm you understand the movement correctly AND that you can demo it in a way clients can follow. This is also a valuable opportunity to get direct feedback on your cueing, your form, and your teaching style.

  4. Build Them Into Your Class Design

    Once you're signed off, include at least 2 PD Warrior exercises in every class you design — for both Fighter and Champion formats. Vary which exercises you use across sessions so clients are always getting neuroplastic challenge through variety and novelty.

Combining PD Warrior's clinically designed neuroactive movements with the cardiovascular intensity, dual-tasking, amplitude training, and community of a B4H boxing class creates something greater than either program alone.

Where PD Warrior provides specific, targeted neuroactive exercises built and tested by specialist physiotherapists, B4H provides the energy, the music, the community, and the boxing framework that makes clients want to keep coming back. Together, they address the full picture — neurological, physical, social, and emotional.

When you add a PD Warrior exercise to your class, you're not just following a B4H policy — you're delivering one of the most evidence-backed Parkinson's interventions in the world inside a class that clients love. That's a powerful combination.
