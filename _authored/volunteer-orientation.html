Volunteers are there to help you run a safe, fun, and high-energy class. This orientation walks you through how to put volunteers to work, where to recruit them, and the class structure they'll help you deliver — so every volunteer arrives ready to make a real difference for the people in your gym.

What you'll learn

  • How to involve volunteers so they help you run a safe, fun, and high-energy class.
  • Which volunteer roles matter most — and the safety limits around them.
  • Where to recruit volunteers from in your community.
  • The four segments of every class, and the difference between Fighter and Champion classes.

Section 01

Working With Volunteers

Volunteers are there to help you run a safe, fun, and high-energy class. Please get them as involved as possible. Once they are familiar with your gym, put them to work in the roles below.

Setup & takedown

Once they are familiar with your gym, have them help with setup and takedown of equipment.

One-on-one support

Having them work one-on-one with a specific student is the top priority — a client having an off day, a client with a DBS, someone at higher fall risk or unstable, or a client experiencing cognitive slowness.

Cognitive games

Show them the box of cognitive games to go around and use with students during class — flash cards, math cards, and similar.

Encourage the voice

Challenge them to find ways to get students to use their voice more in class.

Run a warm up

Have them run a warm up if they are interested.

Safety

Volunteers may hold pads only if they are trained to do so. Safety is our first priority, so please don't encourage untrained volunteers to hold pads.

Coach's Corner

It's important to review volunteer expectations with family members, especially in lower-functioning classes — you may need them to help when you are low on staff or volunteers.

Section 02

Where To Find Volunteers

Volunteers can come from several places in your community. Match the source to the class you need to staff, and always screen for willingness and ability to work with our population.

Retired seniors

Retired seniors who have an interest in giving back and are relatively healthy, depending on what class you need them in.

University & college students

Reach out to university departments that have placement programs — Kinesiology, Neuroscience, Fitness and Recreation — to see if they need placement opportunities. Most do.

High school students

Most high school students need forty (40) hours of volunteer work. Screen them first to see how willing and able they are to work with our population, and stress the importance of their commitment.

Section 03

How Classes Are Structured

Our classes are divided into four segments. The goals of each segment are the same for both Fighter (high functioning) and Champion (low functioning) classes.

  1. Warm Up

    The class opens by getting the body moving and the mind engaged.

  2. Functional Exercises

    Movement work that builds the strength, balance, and coordination students use in daily life.

  3. Boxing

    Boxing technique and drills at the heart of the class.

  4. Cool Down

    The class closes by bringing the body back down and settling students before they leave.

Fighter classes

Fighter classes consist of higher functioning participants and allow more freedom with exercise selection.

Champion classes

Champion classes require more careful exercise selection, more modifications, and a slower, more consistent pace.

Key takeaways

  • Volunteers help you run a safe, fun, and high-energy class — get them as involved as possible.
  • Working one-on-one with a specific student is the top-priority volunteer role.
  • Safety first: volunteers hold pads only if trained, and never encourage untrained volunteers to do so.
  • Recruit from retired seniors, university and college placement programs, and high school students — and always screen them first.
  • Every class has four segments — warm up, functional exercises, boxing, and cool down — with the same goals in Fighter and Champion classes.
  • Review volunteer expectations with family members, especially in lower-functioning classes where you may need their help.
  • Ask every volunteer to watch the orientation video in full before they start.
