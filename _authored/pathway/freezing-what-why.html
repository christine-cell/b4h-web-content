Freezing can feel like your feet are suddenly glued to the floor. This presentation explains what freezing is, why it happens, when it tends to show up — and how you can fight back.

Freezing: What, Why, When & How

While you watch

Freezing of gait (a sudden, short inability to start or keep walking, even though you want to move) is one of the most challenging symptoms of Parkinson’s. It is a brain circuit problem — not a strength or motivation problem. As you watch, think about:

  • What freezing looks and feels like for you.
  • Why it happens in the brain.
  • When it shows up most — starting to walk, turning, doorways, crowds, or walking and talking at the same time.
  • How cues and strategies can help you get moving again.

For all the tips and strategies in writing, go to the next lesson: Overview on freezing and what to do.

Your action this week

Notice where and when freezing happens for you. Write down your top three trigger spots or situations — they are where you’ll practise your strategies.
