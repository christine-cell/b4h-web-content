Why diet matters in Parkinson’s

Nutrition plays a crucial role in managing Parkinson’s disease. While there is no cure, research consistently shows that the right diet can slow disease progression, improve symptoms and boost overall well-being. Among the different ways of eating, the Mediterranean diet stands out as the best for Parkinson’s because it is anti-inflammatory, protects the brain, and is good for your gut.

What is the Mediterranean diet?

The Mediterranean diet is a whole-food, nutrient-rich way of eating inspired by the traditional diets of countries around the Mediterranean Sea. It focuses on:

Healthy fats

Olive oil, nuts and fatty fish provide omega-3s, which support brain health.

Lean proteins

Fish, poultry, legumes and moderate dairy support muscle and nerve function.

Fruits & vegetables

A variety of colourful, antioxidant-rich produce helps fight oxidative stress (a kind of wear and tear on your cells).

Whole grains

Brown rice, quinoa and whole wheat provide fibre and steady energy.

Herbs & spices

Natural anti-inflammatories like turmeric, garlic and rosemary add flavour without added salt.

Moderate red wine & dairy

Limited amounts — particularly fermented dairy like yogurt — contribute to gut health.

Keep to a minimum

Processed foods and red meat. Cutting back on ultra-processed foods lowers inflammation.

What the research says

Slows the progression of Parkinson’s

A study published in Movement Disorders (2021) found that people with Parkinson’s who followed a Mediterranean diet, or the similar MIND diet, had slower disease progression and better mobility over time.

Supports brain health

The Mediterranean diet is high in polyphenols, omega-3 fatty acids and antioxidants, which protect nerve cells from oxidative stress. Research in Neurology (2018) linked this diet with better thinking skills and delayed neurodegeneration (breakdown of nerve cells) in people with Parkinson’s.

Improves gut health and reduces inflammation

There is a strong gut–brain connection in Parkinson’s. The Mediterranean diet promotes a healthy gut microbiome, reducing gut-related inflammation that may contribute to Parkinson’s symptoms. Studies in Parkinsonism & Related Disorders (2020) found this diet improves gut function and reduces constipation — a common Parkinson’s symptom.

Supports dopamine

Dopamine-making nerve cells are damaged in Parkinson’s. Nutrients like B vitamins, folate and polyphenols found in Mediterranean foods help protect these cells and support dopamine production. A study in The American Journal of Clinical Nutrition (2017) showed that a diet rich in leafy greens, olive oil and fish reduced cognitive decline and preserved dopamine levels in aging adults.

Boosts heart and muscle health

Parkinson’s can affect your heart, blood vessels and muscles. The Mediterranean diet’s focus on heart-healthy fats, lean proteins and anti-inflammatory foods supports better circulation, muscle strength and overall function.

How to get started

  1. Put whole, fresh foods first

    Build meals around fresh produce, whole grains and healthy fats.

  2. Cook with olive oil

    Replace butter or margarine with extra virgin olive oil.

  3. Eat more fish and legumes

    Aim for at least two servings of fatty fish (salmon, sardines) per week.

  4. Snack smart

    Choose nuts, seeds and yogurt instead of processed snacks.

  5. Cut back on sugar and processed foods

    Swap refined grains and sweets for fruit and whole foods.

  6. Stay hydrated

    Drink plenty of water and herbal teas, and limit sugary drinks.

Final thoughts

The Mediterranean diet is more than just food — it’s a lifestyle that promotes long-term health, clear thinking and overall well-being for people with Parkinson’s. With strong scientific backing, it’s the most effective, sustainable and enjoyable way to nourish both body and brain.

If you or a loved one has Parkinson’s, adopting the Mediterranean diet can be a powerful step in managing symptoms and improving quality of life.

Talk to your care team

This lesson is for education only and isn’t medical advice. Check with your doctor, neurologist or pharmacist before starting a supplement or making a big change to your diet or medication.

My Mediterranean meal plan

Just some ideas to get you started! This plan is made for 1 person — simply double it for 2.

Breakfast: Berry & Chia Overnight Oats

Make-ahead; keeps in the fridge for 3–4 days.

  • ½ cup oats
  • 1 tbsp chia seeds
  • ½ cup almond milk
  • ½ cup mixed berries (blueberries, strawberries, raspberries)
  • 1 tbsp walnuts
  • A drizzle of honey (optional)

Mix and refrigerate overnight.

Or have a yogurt parfait instead: ¾ cup yogurt, ½ cup berries, 1 tsp chia seeds, ½ cup granola and 1 scoop collagen protein powder. Optional add-on: 1 tsp nut butter.

Lunch: Lentil & Spinach Soup

Make a big batch and freeze portions.

  • 1 cup red lentils
  • 4 cups vegetable broth
  • 1 can diced tomatoes
  • 1 carrot, chopped
  • 1 celery stalk, chopped
  • 1 tsp turmeric
  • 1 tsp garlic
  • ½ tsp ginger
  • Black pepper
  • Optional: ½ lb ground turkey or chicken
  • 2 cups spinach (added at the end)

Simmer for 30 minutes. Blend if you like.

Dinner: Baked Salmon with Roasted Vegetables

Easy sheet-pan meal.

  • 1 salmon fillet
  • 1 cup broccoli
  • ½ cup sweet potato, diced
  • 1 tbsp olive oil
  • ½ tsp turmeric, ½ tsp garlic powder

Bake at 375°F (190°C) for 20 minutes.

Snack: Turmeric Golden Smoothie

  • ½ banana
  • 1 cup almond milk
  • ½ tsp turmeric
  • ½ tsp cinnamon
  • 1 tbsp ground flaxseed
  • A handful of cut mango or pineapple

Breakfast: Avocado Toast with Hemp Seeds

5-minute meal.

  • 1 slice whole-grain toast
  • 3 scrambled eggs or a tofu scramble
  • ½ avocado, mashed
  • 1 tsp hemp seeds
  • A squeeze of lemon

Lunch: Quinoa & Chickpea Salad

Make-ahead; keeps in the fridge for 3–4 days.

  • 1 cup cooked quinoa
  • ½ cup chickpeas
  • ½ cucumber, diced
  • ½ bell pepper, diced
  • 1 tbsp olive oil and lemon dressing — or my new favourite, Thai peanut dressing. YUM! (Recipe below.)

Dinner: Turkey & Spinach Meatballs with Zucchini Noodles

The meatballs freeze well.

  • 1 lb ground turkey
  • 1 cup veggies (e.g. peppers, onions, mushrooms)
  • 1 cup spinach, chopped
  • ½ cup almond flour
  • 1 tsp oregano, 1 tsp garlic powder

Bake the meatballs at 375°F (190°C) for 20 minutes. Serve with zucchini noodles and marinara — or, instead of zucchini noodles, use lentil noodles or the noodles of your choice. Top with 2 tbsp parmesan cheese, or dairy-free Boursin.

Snack

Walnuts and dark chocolate (70% cacao), or pitted dates with nuts.

Breakfast: Greek Yogurt & Walnut Parfait

5-minute meal.

  • ¾ cup plain Greek yogurt, or dairy-free protein yogurt (preferred)
  • ½ cup mixed berries
  • 1 tbsp walnuts — or, my preference, a little nut butter of your choice
  • 1 scoop collagen protein powder
  • A drizzle of honey

Lunch: Carrot Ginger Soup

Make a big batch and freeze portions.

  • 4 large carrots, chopped
  • 1 inch of ginger, grated
  • 3 cups vegetable broth
  • 1 small onion, diced
  • 5 oz grilled tofu or baked salmon on the side

Simmer for 20 minutes and blend.

Dinner: Garlic Shrimp with Brown Rice & Asparagus

Simple stovetop meal.

  • 1 cup cooked brown rice
  • ½ cup shrimp, sautéed with 1 tsp olive oil and garlic
  • 1 cup asparagus, roasted

Snack

Hummus and veggies.

Breakfast: Oatmeal with Almond Butter & Blueberries

Quick and filling.

  • ½ cup oats
  • 1 tbsp almond butter
  • ½ cup blueberries
  • 1 tbsp ground flaxseed

Lunch: Tuna & Avocado Wrap

Easy to assemble and portable.

  • 1 whole-grain wrap
  • ½ avocado, mashed
  • ½ cup tuna
  • A handful of spinach

Dinner: Slow Cooker Chocolate Chili with Sweet Potato

Freezer-friendly meal.

  • 1 lb ground beef (grass-fed) or turkey — I pan-fry the meat for a few minutes first to lock in some flavour, then put it in the slow cooker
  • 3 cloves garlic
  • 1 bell pepper, 1 small onion, and mushrooms or zucchini, cut in small cubes
  • 1 can diced tomatoes
  • 2 tbsp tomato paste
  • 1 tbsp olive or avocado oil
  • 1 tbsp chili powder
  • 1 tbsp cacao powder
  • Salt and pepper to taste

Cook in the slow cooker on high for 3–4 hours, or on low for 6.

Snack

Pumpkin seeds and green tea.

Meal prep and freezer options (for 2 people)

  • Make ahead and freeze: lentil soup, turkey meatballs, carrot ginger soup, chicken stew.
  • Make ahead and refrigerate (3–4 days): overnight oats, quinoa salad.
  • Easy to assemble last-minute: smoothies, avocado toast, Greek yogurt parfait.

5-day gluten-free meal plan for 2 people

Balanced, easy-to-prep meals with variety and nutrition.

  • Base: 2 cups Greek yogurt
  • Toppings: 1 cup gluten-free granola, 1 cup mixed berries, 2 tbsp honey

Lunch: Turkey & Avocado Wraps

  • Wraps: 2 gluten-free wraps
  • Filling: 8 oz turkey slices, 1 avocado, 1 cup shredded lettuce, 4 tbsp mustard or mayo

Dinner: Beef & Veggie Stir-Fry with Cauliflower Rice

  • Protein: 12 oz sliced beef
  • Veggies: 2 cups bell peppers, 1 cup snap peas, ½ onion
  • Sauce: coconut aminos, garlic, ginger
  • Serve with: 2 cups cauliflower rice

Breakfast: Peanut Butter Banana Smoothie

Blend: 2 bananas, 2 cups almond milk, 2 tbsp peanut butter, 2 tbsp chia seeds.

Lunch: Mediterranean Quinoa Bowls

  • Base: 2 cups cooked quinoa
  • Add-ins: 1 cup cherry tomatoes, ½ cup feta, ½ cup olives, 1 cup chickpeas, 2 tbsp olive oil, lemon juice

Dinner: Lemon Herb Chicken with Mashed Sweet Potatoes

  • Protein: 2 grilled chicken breasts, with the spices of your choice
  • Sides: 2 mashed sweet potatoes (butter, garlic), 2 cups steamed green beans

Breakfast: Mushroom & Goat Cheese Omelette

  • Eggs: 4 eggs or a tofu scramble
  • Filling: 1 cup mushrooms, ¼ cup goat cheese
  • Side: 2 slices gluten-free toast

Lunch: Tuna Salad Lettuce Wraps

  • Mix: 2 cans tuna, 4 tbsp mayo, 2 tbsp mustard, ½ cup diced celery
  • Serve in: romaine leaves

Dinner: Shrimp (or Scallop) Curry with Rice

  • Protein: 12 oz shrimp
  • Sauce: 1 can coconut milk, 1 tbsp curry paste, 1 tbsp soy sauce or coconut aminos (gluten-free)
  • Veggies: 1 bell pepper, ½ onion, a handful of spinach added at the end
  • Serve with: 2 cups cooked jasmine rice

Breakfast: Almond Butter & Berry Chia Pudding

  • Base: ½ cup chia seeds, 2 cups almond milk, 1 tsp vanilla, 1 tbsp maple syrup
  • Toppings: 1 cup mixed berries, 2 tbsp almond butter

Lunch: Chicken & Hummus Lettuce Wraps

  • Protein: 2 grilled chicken breasts
  • Toppings: ½ cup hummus, 1 cup shredded carrots
  • Wrap in: romaine leaves

Dinner: Stuffed Peppers with Ground Turkey & Quinoa

Filling: Stuff 2 bell peppers with 8 oz ground turkey, 1 cup cooked quinoa and ½ cup tomato sauce. Bake at 375°F (190°C) for 25 minutes.

Bonus recipe

A rich, creamy and flavourful sauce — perfect for dipping, drizzling over stir-fries, or as a marinade.

Ingredients

  • ½ cup natural peanut butter (unsweetened)
  • ¼ cup coconut aminos
  • 1 tbsp lime juice (or rice vinegar for a tangier kick)
  • 1 tbsp maple syrup (or honey)
  • 1–2 tsp sriracha (adjust to taste)
  • 1 tsp grated ginger (or ½ tsp ground ginger)
  • 1 clove garlic, minced
  • ¼ cup warm water (adjust for the thickness you like)
  • 1 tbsp toasted sesame oil
  • 1 tsp fish sauce (optional, for extra savoury depth)

Method

  1. In a medium bowl, whisk together the peanut butter and coconut aminos until smooth.
  2. Add the lime juice, maple syrup, sriracha, ginger and garlic. Whisk well.
  3. Gradually stir in warm water until you reach the thickness you like.
  4. Finish with the toasted sesame oil and fish sauce (if using).
  5. Taste and adjust — more lime juice for tang, maple syrup for sweetness, or sriracha for heat.

Ways to use it

  • As a dipping sauce for spring rolls or grilled chicken
  • Drizzled over stir-fried vegetables or noodles
  • As a marinade for tofu, shrimp or beef
  • Tossed with shredded cabbage for a quick Thai-inspired slaw

Keeps in the fridge for 2 weeks in a sealed jar.

Key takeaways

  • The Mediterranean diet is built on healthy fats, lean proteins, colourful produce, whole grains, and herbs and spices.
  • Research links it to slower Parkinson’s progression, better brain and gut health, and less constipation.
  • Start simple: cook with olive oil, eat fish and legumes, snack on nuts and seeds, and drink water.
