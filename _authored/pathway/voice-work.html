Why your voice and face need a workout

Parkinson’s affects more than your arms and legs. It also affects the structures and movements of your facial expressions and your vocal cords. Your voice may become quieter, slower or harder to understand — though not everyone will experience this.

The more you use your voice, the stronger it will be. Your voice box is a muscle too, so keep it in good shape, just as much as the rest of your body. The sooner you get to work on keeping your face and voice active, the better.

Warm up first

Because your voice box is made of muscles, you can strain it. Never skip your vocal warm-up and jump straight into loud exercises.

Your facial expressions guide

Add these facial exercises to your daily routine. They can help to improve your facial expressions. Try your best to look as silly as me!

  1. Pucker up!

  2. Big smile

    Make an exaggerated smile showing your teeth.

  3. Drink from a straw

    Pretend to drink from a straw and breathe in. Bonus points if you open your eyes as wide as possible!

  4. Tongue stretch

    Stretch your tongue up and down, and from side to side.

  5. Puff your cheeks

    Blow out your cheeks, both at the same time or one after the other.

Key takeaways

  • Parkinson’s can affect your voice and the muscles of your face.
  • The more you use your voice, the stronger it will be.
  • Always warm up your voice before loud exercises.
  • Five simple facial exercises, done daily, can help your expressions.
