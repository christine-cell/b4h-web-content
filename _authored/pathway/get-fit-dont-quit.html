Why intensity is key

Reverses brain damage

Yale University research (2024) showed high-intensity exercise can reverse neurodegeneration (the loss of brain cells) and increase dopamine-producing neurons — not just slow decline, but actually restore brain health.

Intensity matters most

It’s not just about moving — it’s about how hard you work. High-intensity exercise (80–85% of your maximum heart rate) triggers neuroprotection (protection of your brain cells) that moderate exercise doesn’t achieve.

Grows brain cells

High-intensity exercise increases BDNF (Brain-Derived Neurotrophic Factor) — like fertilizer for your brain cells. It helps them survive, grow and make new connections.

No drug can do this

Current medications only treat symptoms. High-intensity exercise is the only intervention proven to potentially slow, stop or reverse disease progression at the cellular level.

Inactivity makes it worse

Animal studies show that immobilization (not moving) worsens Parkinson’s deficits. Not exercising at high intensity means missing the chance to fight back in your brain and nerves.

The “talk test”

You should be able to speak in short sentences, but not sing a song. That’s your target intensity — challenging, but something you can keep up for short periods.

From a neurologist

“The medications we have available are only for symptomatic treatment. They do not change the disease course. But exercise seems to go one step beyond and protect the brain at the neuronal level.”

— Dr. Sule Tinaz, Associate Professor of Neurology, Yale School of Medicine
