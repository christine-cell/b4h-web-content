Why rotation matters

We all need more rotation — starting with the mid-back, or thoracic region. For people with Parkinson’s, a very rigid (stiff) core is especially common, and the longer you wait, the harder it becomes to get rotation back.

Your lower back is not designed to rotate. When your mid-back stops turning, other parts of your spine try to compensate, and that sets the stage for injury. Keeping your thoracic region mobile protects your entire spine — and relieves strain on your lower back and shoulders. Read more in VIGOR.

Safety first

Move slowly and only as far as feels comfortable. Start with the seated video if you’re unsure of your balance, and take your time getting down to and up from the floor.

Your action this week

Include rotation in every workout, and try a seated or standing mid-back twist every day.
