What is light therapy?

Light therapy, or photobiomodulation (PBM), is the non-thermal (no heat) delivery of waves of light energy with a therapeutic benefit. At the right wavelength, intensity and exposure time, light aimed at the body’s tissue sets off a series of biochemical events. These can result in chronic pain relief, reduced inflammation, and even increased dopamine and serotonin production in the colon.

By treating the gut with laser light, SYMBYX is able to effect change in a person’s microbiome (the community of bacteria living in your gut), which ultimately leads to improved brain function.

SYMBYX is an Australian medical technology company that develops light therapies for chronic diseases and painful inflammatory conditions. A key focus is using light therapy (PBM) to reduce Parkinson’s symptoms. They treat mainly through the gut microbiome, because of the well-researched link between the gut and the brain (the gut–brain axis).

PBM is not a new area of medicine. The 1903 Nobel Prize in medicine was won by a Danish doctor for using ultraviolet blue and red light to treat smallpox and TB scars. Today, there are over 4,000 peer-reviewed scientific studies on light’s safe, protective and restorative properties for a range of health problems.

The right dose of light (wavelength, application time and intensity) is critical — too much or too little has less effect. SYMBYX lasers deliver specifically calibrated doses using super-pulsing technology, without heating. They are non-invasive, painless and portable.

In Parkinson’s, it is not only the loss of dopamine-producing brain cells, but also problems with the mitochondria (the part of every living cell that produces the energy needed for daily life), that cause movement symptoms and non-motor symptoms — such as reduced balance, thinking ability and sense of smell, sleep problems, depression and loss of motivation. According to SYMBYX, light therapy energizes cell mitochondria and can help with the loss of cellular energy common in Parkinson’s.

Most people don’t realize that the colon can produce both dopamine and serotonin — chemical messengers the brain needs to work well. SYMBYX says PBM to the gut reduces inflammation and promotes the metabolism of short-chain fatty acids (SCFAs), which in turn influences the production of more dopamine and serotonin.

People with Parkinson’s are reporting improvements in mobility, thinking, dynamic balance, sleep, motivation and fine motor skills after using the PD Care laser. A study in BMC Neurology (July 2021) noted “no adverse side effects” and described PBM as a “safe and potentially effective treatment for Parkinson’s disease symptoms,” with improvements maintained for as long as treatment continued — for up to one year.

How the laser is used

SYMBYX’s treatment protocol treats the stomach (abdomen) and the neck. Each session takes 20 minutes, 3 times a week. Their research shows the best results come from using the laser three times a week, so it is ideally done at home.

  1. Turn it on

    Press the button on the base of the laser. You’ll hear 4 short beeps and see a flashing light.

  2. Treat 9 spots on your stomach (18 minutes)

    Press the laser onto bare skin with enough pressure to turn it on — you’ll hear one beep. It then beeps every minute. Hold it on each of the 9 spots for 2 full beeps (2 minutes × 9 spots = 18 minutes), in a rough grid. The top 3 spots are just under the ribs, and the bottom 3 are just above the pelvis. The laser must touch your skin directly.

  3. Treat your neck (2 minutes)

    Press the laser against your neck at the base of your skull for 2 beeps (2 minutes). Only do this on days you use the laser.

  4. Turn it off

    Use the button on the base to switch it off.
