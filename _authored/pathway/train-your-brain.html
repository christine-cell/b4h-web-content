Train your brain while you move. These progressive dual-tasking challenges work your mind and body at the same time — just like real life does — to help you feel safer and more confident on your feet.

What you’ll do

  • Understand why dual-tasking (doing a physical and a mental task at once) matters for balance with Parkinson’s.
  • Work through three levels — Gentle Start, Building Up and Full Challenge — pairing a balance exercise with a brain task.
  • Try a fun brain challenge at each level while you move.
  • Pick up Christine’s coaching tips for getting the most out of dual-tasking.

Why dual-tasking matters for Parkinson’s

In real life, you never just walk. You walk and talk. You stand and search for something on a shelf. You step over a curb and answer a question.

For people living with Parkinson’s, splitting attention between a physical task and a mental task is one of the biggest contributors to freezing (when your feet suddenly feel stuck) and falls. Your brain has to work harder to manage movement that used to be automatic.

The good news: dual-tasking is trainable. The more you practise it, the better your brain gets at managing both — and the safer and more confident you feel in everyday life.

Safety first

Before you start: stand beside your chair with one hand resting on the back for support. Do your balance exercise while you complete each challenge. If you need to sit at any point — sit. Safety first, challenge second.

Level 1

Level 1 — Gentle Start

Seated or standing with both hands on the chair. Simple brain tasks with easy physical movement.

  1. Sit to stand + count backwards from 20

    Physical task: rise from your chair slowly and lower back down. 10 repetitions. Use the armrests if needed.

    Add this while you move: say the numbers out loud as you stand and sit. 20… 19… 18… If you lose count, just restart — that’s the whole point.

  2. Weight shift side to side + name the months

    Physical task: standing with your hands on the chair, slowly shift your weight to your right foot, then your left. Alternate for 20 shifts.

    Add this while you move: say one month out loud with each shift. January, February… see if you get through all 12.

Level 1 brain challenges

Do your sit-to-stands while you count down.

  1. Start at 20. Say one number about every 3 seconds, counting down to zero.
  2. Stand up on odd numbers, sit down on even numbers.
  3. Keep going until you reach zero. Well done! Rest, then go again.

Ask a partner to call out the numbers so you have to keep up with their pace.

Do your weight shifts side to side while you play.

  1. Ask a partner to lay out 8 matching pairs of cards (16 cards) face down on a table beside you — playing cards work well.
  2. Flip two cards at a time and try to find all the matching pairs.
  3. One flip per weight shift! Count your moves and try to beat your score next time.

Level 2

Level 2 — Building Up

Standing with one hand lightly on the chair. More demanding brain tasks are added.

  1. Side stepping + name animals A to Z

    Physical task: lift your knee up and step over an imaginary obstacle. 10 reps each side, one hand lightly on the chair.

    Add this while you move: one animal per step. Antelope, Bear, Cat, Dog… Say them out loud. It’s harder than it sounds by the time you reach M.

  2. Hip flexion + alternate colours

    Physical task: standing tall, lift one knee to hip height and lower it slowly. Alternate legs for 20 reps total.

    Add this while you move: Red, Blue, Red, Blue… or try cycling Red, Blue, Green. One colour per knee lift. Change the pattern when it gets easy.

Level 2 brain challenges

Stand beside your chair doing hip flexions. Pick a category and say one word at a time out loud. How many can you name in 60 seconds?

  • Animals
  • Foods
  • Countries
  • Things in a kitchen
  • Things that are round
  • People’s names starting with M

Ask a partner to time you and count your words. Great work!

Stand and do your side stepping.

  1. Your partner calls out a colour pattern using RED, BLUE, GOLD and GREEN — start with just one colour.
  2. Repeat the pattern back in the same order.
  3. Each round, your partner adds one more colour to the pattern. How far can you go?

Level 3

Level 3 — Full Challenge

Standing with your fingertips barely touching the chair. Demanding brain tasks that need real concentration.

  1. Wall squats + subtract 7 from 100

    Physical task: back against the wall, feet shoulder-width apart. Slide down slowly. Hold for 10 seconds, then return to standing. 5 reps.

    Add this while you move: 100, 93, 86, 79… Say each answer out loud as you hold the squat. One number per second. Don’t stop moving.

  2. Stepping back + tell a story

    Physical task: facing your chair with your hands on the seat, take a big step backward with one leg and return. 10 each side.

    Add this while you move: describe your morning out loud while stepping — what you had for breakfast, what you saw, who you spoke to. Keep talking, keep moving.

Level 3 brain challenges

This is a classic dual-task brain challenge. Stand and do your hip flexions. Name the colour the word is written in — not what the word says. It’s trickier than it looks.

  1. Ask a partner to write the words RED, BLUE, GREEN, GOLD and PURPLE on cards, most of them in a marker of a different colour (for example, the word BLUE written in red).
  2. Your partner shows you one card at a time while you keep moving.
  3. Say the ink colour out loud. Keep track of how many you get right in a row.

Christine’s coaching tips for dual-tasking

Christine’s tip

  • If you lose track of the mental task, don’t stop moving. Keep your body going and restart the mental task. That moment of recovery is actually great training.
  • It’s normal to feel frustrated. That frustration means your brain is working. Stick with it.
  • Start at Level 1 even if it feels easy. The goal is quality of movement, not proving you can do Level 3.
  • Do these challenges 3–4 times per week for best results. Progress happens through repetition, not intensity.
  • Laugh when it goes wrong. Seriously — the people in my classes who laugh the most also tend to progress the fastest.

Key takeaways

  • Splitting attention between moving and thinking is a big contributor to freezing and falls in Parkinson’s — and it’s trainable.
  • Always start beside a sturdy chair, and sit whenever you need to.
  • Build up through the levels: Gentle Start, Building Up, Full Challenge.
  • Practise 3–4 times a week. Repetition, not intensity, drives progress.
  • Keep moving when you lose track, and laugh when it goes wrong.
