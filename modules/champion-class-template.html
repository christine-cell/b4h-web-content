Champion Class TemplateModèle de cours Champion

7 min read Updated 2026

The Champion class delivers the same therapeutic goals as the Fighter class — with intentional modifications woven through every segment. The exercises are familiar; the adaptations are what make this class safe and effective for clients who need more support, with a chair available at all times and a heart-rate target of 70–80%. This template walks you through the full timed flow: warm up, high-intensity circuit and boxing, and cool down.

What you'll learn

  • Understand how the Champion format mirrors Fighter exercises while modifying every station for seated and supported participation.
  • Run the three timed segments — Warm Up (7–10 min), High Intensity (32–35 min), and Cool Down (5–10 min) — with the right pacing.
  • Deliver a warm up that always includes BIG movements and voice, using three delivery options plus seated voice exercises.
  • Coach a six-station circuit and six boxing combinations, applying the correct chair modification at each one.
  • Close the class with a cool down that eases blood-pressure fluctuations through stretching, breathing, and ball release work.

Section 01

Class Structure at a Glance

The Champion class is built from the same exercises as Fighter, but a chair must always be within reach and modifications are the default rather than the exception. Fighter runs higher intensity, circuit-based, and standing-focused for Stage 1–2 clients; Champion keeps the same exercises with chair-supported and seated options for Stage 2–3 clients.

Warm Up 7–10 min

Big movements and voice exercises — all with a chair accessible throughout.

High Intensity 32–35 min

Modified functional circuits plus boxing combinations at 70–80% of target heart rate.

Cool Down 5–10 min

Stretching, breathing, and myofascial ball release work to ease the transition out of intensity.

Section 02

Warm Up · 7–10 Minutes

Champion warm-ups use the same exercises as Fighter, with a chair accessible for every student. Encourage standing as much as possible, but every exercise can be done seated if needed. Two elements are non-negotiable in every Champion warm-up: BIG movements and voice.

Coach's Corner

Space note: If stations have been set up, use the whole gym as much as possible for the warm up and other exercises — get clients moving through the space rather than staying in one spot.

  1. Option 1 — Go Big, Moving Around the Gym

    Same exercises as Fighter, but a chair must be accessible for every student. Encourage standing as much as possible; all exercises can be done seated if needed. A chair stays available at all times.

    • Big Walk — around the square or gym perimeter
    • High Knees — exaggerated lift, arms swinging
    • Butt Kicks — upright posture, deliberate movement
    • Monster Walk — BIG lateral steps with full arm extension

  2. Option 2 — Go Big, Standing in Spot

    Same exercises in place. Have clients place hands on the wall for balance assistance as needed. Wall or chair support is encouraged. Use a chair when clients experience any of the following:

    • Fatigue
    • Dizziness
    • An OFF period
    • Back pain
    • Lack of strength to stand for an extended period

  3. Option 3 — PWR Warm Up, Seated Coming soon

    A seated warm-up option using PWR! principles for clients who need a fully seated class. This resource is coming soon — check the Instructor Forum for updates when it is added.

Coach's Corner

You have creative freedom here. Add your own warm-up elements as long as BIG movements and voice are always included — those two are non-negotiable in every Champion warm up.

Vowel Sounds

A, E, I, O, U — full face involvement, holding each sound as long as possible.

Alphabet Letters in the Air

Draw each letter of the alphabet as BIG as they can while saying it out loud — a great seated exercise for amplitude and voice at the same time.

Section 03

High Intensity · 32–35 Minutes

The Champion format uses the same exercises as Fighter, with purposeful modifications at every station. Small adjustments make each exercise accessible without sacrificing therapeutic value. The two cards below show how each Fighter movement maps to its Champion modification.

Fighter version

  • PWR ups from standing
  • Double 007 — full standing rotation
  • Lateral leg raises — standing
  • Full burpees
  • Core — floor or standing
  • Scarf toss in lunge — standing

Champion modification

  • PWR ups from seated position
  • Double 007 — seated or supported rotation
  • Lateral leg raises using a chair
  • Modified burpees with chair, finishing with a PWR up
  • Core — seated, breath and posture focus
  • Scarf toss in lunge — chair supported

Sample Circuit · 6 Stations

Six rounds, two minutes each, with 30 seconds to 1 minute of rest between rounds.

  1. 1. PWR! Ups from Seated 2 min

    Full amplitude on the rise — chest open, arms extended, tall finish. The movement starts from the seat, not a standing position.

    Champion modification: Start seated. Focus on a powerful push through the legs and full extension at the top.

  2. 2. Double 007 — Trunk Rotation 2 min

    Add a cognitive dual-task: count backwards from 100 in 3s, or call out vegetables starting with A through Z.

    Champion modification: Seated or with chair support. Eyes track the hands. Don't sacrifice the rotation for speed.

  3. 3. Lateral Leg Raises 2 min

    Hip abduction with an upright trunk — don't let the body lean into the raise.

    Champion modification: Use a chair for support. Focus on a controlled lift and return — both directions are therapeutic.

  4. 4. Modified Burpees + PWR! Up Finish 2 min

    The chair replaces the floor component — clients push up from a chair, step back (or march in place), then finish with a full PWR! up.

    Champion modification: Chair throughout. The finish — standing tall with full arm extension — is non-negotiable. That's the therapeutic payoff.

  5. 5. Core — Seated, Breath & Posture 2 min

    Seated core work focused on breath coordination and postural awareness. Think tall spine, diaphragmatic breathing, and gentle trunk activation.

    Champion modification: Fully seated. Focus on quality of breath and posture rather than intensity. This is a recovery station within the circuit.

  6. 6. Scarf Toss in Lunge — Switch Legs 2 min

    Lunge position — scarf toss and catch. Switch legs. Balance, coordination, and reaction speed all in one.

    Champion modification: Chair nearby or hands on the wall for balance during the lunge. Reduce lunge depth as needed — keep the scarf toss BIG.

Coach's Corner

Struggling student? If a student can't keep up or is struggling with instructions, have another instructor or volunteer work with them one-on-one. Break down the movements — focus on legs first, then reintroduce arms. Never leave a struggling client to figure it out alone.

Boxing Combinations

Champion boxing uses a slightly different combination structure — varied rhythm, different patterns, and built-in recovery intervals. Adapt timing and rounds based on your group.

  1. Round 1 — Jab · Cross · Jab · Cross + Switch Stance

    Double the basics, then a stance switch. Gets both sides working from the start.

  2. Round 2 — Jab · Cross · Big Backstep · Knee

    The backstep adds footwork and spatial awareness before the knee — a big balance and coordination challenge.

  3. Round 3 — Jab · Cross · Cross · Jab

    Reversed rhythm — ends with the jab instead of the cross. A cognitive challenge inside a familiar combination pattern.

  4. Round 4 — Body · Body · Head · Head

    Level changes — punch low then high. Requires trunk flexion and extension alternating with each combination.

  5. Round 5 — 10 Seconds On · 10 Seconds Off · × 3

    Interval burst — maximum effort for 10 seconds, full rest for 10 seconds, repeated 3 times. Short enough to push hard, long enough to recover. Great for clients managing fatigue.

  6. Round 6 — Big Boxing Freestyle

    Open round — clients choose their own combinations. Freedom within the framework: their choice, their confidence, their ownership of the movement.

Coach's Corner

Instructor's discretion: Feel free to change and advance combinations with groups that are more comfortable. The combinations above are starting points — read your room and challenge clients appropriately.

Section 04

Cool Down · 5–10 Minutes

Focus on 3–5 stretches based on what you worked in class. For Champion clients, the cool down is especially important — many experience blood-pressure fluctuations and need a gradual transition out of intensity.

Yoga Techniques

Seated or standing yoga poses — especially chest openers and hip flexor stretches. Modify all poses for chair support.

Breathing Exercises

Extended exhale breathing, box breathing, or diaphragmatic work. Especially beneficial for voice, respiratory function, and nervous-system regulation.

Ball Releases

Therapy or massage ball on the back, chest, feet, or shoulders. Seated ball work is fully effective — no need to stand.

Targeted Stretches

Based on what you used in class — hip flexors after sit-stands, chest after boxing, hamstrings after leg raises.

Good to know

A Champion class ideas video is available to watch — use it to observe pacing, chair usage, modification style, and how the instructor maintains energy and connection with the group throughout.

Key takeaways

  • Champion uses the same exercises as Fighter, modified for chair-supported and seated participation — a chair is available at all times.
  • The class runs three timed segments: Warm Up (7–10 min), High Intensity (32–35 min at 70–80% target heart rate), and Cool Down (5–10 min).
  • Every warm up must include BIG movements and voice; beyond that you have creative freedom to add your own elements.
  • Work the six-station circuit at two minutes per station with short rests, and apply each station's chair modification while protecting the therapeutic finish.
  • Boxing combinations build in recovery intervals and freestyle — advance them at your discretion for more comfortable groups.
  • Never rush the cool down: it eases the blood-pressure fluctuations common in Champion clients.
