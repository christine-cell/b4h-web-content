Every Boxing4Health class follows the same three-phase shape — a warm-up, a high-intensity block, and a cool-down — and every client is placed into one of three fighter classifications after their assessment. This overview shows you how the phases and their timings fit together, and how to tell a Fighter, a Champion, and a Champion Plus apart.

What you'll learn

  • The three phases of a B4H class and the timing for each — warm-up, high intensity, and cool-down.
  • The three fighter classifications — Fighter, Champion, and Champion Plus — and what makes each class format different.
  • How the client assessment determines which class a student is placed in.

Placement

The Three Fighter Classifications

We have three types of classifications for our fighters: Fighter, Champion, and Champion Plus. Once your assessment with the client is complete, you will then be able to place the student in the appropriate class.

Fighter

Higher-intensity, circuit-based classes. Best for those with low to moderate balance concerns.

Exercises are Parkinson's-specific and include amplitude-based movements and dual tasking. Cognitive exercises are also added in these classes.

Champion

Lower-intensity classes, set up as a group class rather than a circuit class. Champion classes are a mix of Champion and Champion Plus clients.

Champion Plus

Trains alongside Champions in the same lower-intensity group format. See the Champion Plus Program document below for the difference between these two clients.

Key takeaways

  • Every class runs in three phases: warm-up (7–10 min), high intensity (32–35 min), and cool-down (10–15 min).
  • The high-intensity block mixes functional exercises with boxing technique and drills; the cool-down uses gentle stretching and self-massage ball work.
  • There are three fighter classifications: Fighter (higher-intensity, circuit-based), Champion, and Champion Plus (both lower-intensity, group format).
  • Fighter classes suit clients with low to moderate balance concerns and use Parkinson's-specific amplitude movements, dual tasking, and cognitive exercises.
  • Place each student only after completing their assessment, and use the Champion Plus Program document to distinguish Champion from Champion Plus.
