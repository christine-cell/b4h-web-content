Before a new student ever throws a punch, you need a working sense of where they are — high or low functioning — so you can place them in the right trial class. You won't have a formal diagnosis in hand, and you don't need one. This lesson walks you through how to screen a prospective student, when to lean on your own judgment, and the assessments that follow once they've had their trial class.

What you'll learn

  • Screen a prospective student over the phone or in person to gauge whether a High or Low functioning trial class suits them best.
  • Use your discretion when you're unsure — book an assessment first or invite them to observe a class.
  • Understand the initial health assessment that every student completes after their trial class, before starting regular classes.
  • Know when a senior should have the same assessment and how to book the virtual assessment.

Good to know

Need a refresher on what each stage looks like? Review Lesson 2 · The 5 Stages before you screen your first prospect.

Section 01

Screening a Prospective Student

Screen a student over the phone or in person with the prospect screening questions so you can properly assess which class — High or Low functioning — they would be best suited to as their Trial Class.

  1. Ask the screening questions

    Work through the prospect screening questions over the phone or in person. Their answers give you a first read on mobility, balance, and daily function.

  2. Match them to a trial class

    Based on what you hear, decide whether a High or Low functioning class is the better fit for their Trial Class.

  3. When you're unsure, look closer

    If you're not confident, book an assessment beforehand, or invite them to come in and watch a class so you can observe how they move and get a better idea of their physical limitations.

Coach's Corner

There's no penalty for taking the extra step. If a phone call leaves you unsure, use your discretion — a short assessment or a class observation tells you far more than a questionnaire alone, and it protects both the student and the class.

Section 02

Assessments After the Trial Class

Once a student has completed their trial class, a full assessment comes next — before they can start regular classes. Every student you see will have different health concerns and abilities, so a complete health assessment matters.

Initial Assessment

Completed after a trial class and before the student can start classes. Because every student arrives with different health concerns and abilities, a full health assessment must be done once they've had their trial.

Parkinson's Assessment

Seniors should have the same assessment if they're concerned about their balance, are a fall risk, or want baseline measurements to track their progress over time.
