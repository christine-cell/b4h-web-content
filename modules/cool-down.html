Section 01

The Cool Down

A cool down of roughly 5–10 minutes is a perfect way to end the class. Stretching and releasing are very important for the Parkinson's population, and this gentle final phase gives the body a chance to unwind after high-intensity work.

Stretching

Lengthen and release the muscles worked during class, helping to reduce tightness and improve flexibility.

Breathing

Guided breathing helps calm the nervous system and transition the body out of an active, elevated state.

Massage Ball Work

Self-massage with therapy balls releases tight, sore muscles and fascia — a simple, effective way to finish.

Safety

Muscle rigidity is relatively common amongst people with Parkinson's. Be mindful of your fighters' limitations when selecting the components of your cool-down session.

Section 02

Coach's Corner

Here are some examples of a cool down you can draw from — stretches, breathing, and massage ball work. Mix and match the components to suit the fighters in front of you, and lean on therapy balls and fascial release wherever you can to help ease rigidity.

Key takeaways

  • End every class with a 5–10 minute cool down — stretching and releasing are very important for the Parkinson's population.
  • Build the cool down around three elements: stretching, breathing, and massage ball work.
  • Muscle rigidity is common with Parkinson's — be mindful of each fighter's limitations when selecting components.
