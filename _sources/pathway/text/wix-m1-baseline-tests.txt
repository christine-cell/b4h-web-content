#1-TUG TEST (back against the chair) Begin by sitting back in a standard arm chair
and identify a line 10 feet away, on the floor. Start with your back against the chair.
Have someone say GO. The time starts and you begin walking to the line and then
back to the chair. Stop timing after you sit back down.

#2-SIT-STAND (5 times) Sit in the middle of the chair. Place each hand on the
opposite shoulder crossed at the wrists. Place your feet flat on the floor. Keep your
back straight and keep your arms against your chest. When someone says the
command GO, the time starts and you rise to a full standing position and then sit
back down again. Repeat 5 times.

#3-STEP UPS (30 sec) Find a sturdy step to use. The bottom step of your stairs works
well too. I want you to step all the way up with both feet (one at a time) on to the
top of the step and back down. You are not stepping over it, just to the top and
back down. Getting one foot up to the top and then the other foot and then back
down counts as one step. Continue for 30 seconds and see how many complete
steps you can get.

#4-DOWN TO THE GROUND For this test you start standing up. Make sure you have
a chair or you are near a couch especially if you have not had experience getting
down to the ground. PLEASE do not try this if you don’t have someone spotting you
or have help to get back up if you get stuck. It’s also recommended to try this on a
soft floor and not hardwood floors on your first attempt.
Your goal is to have yourself timed getting down to the ground onto your belly and
then back up again. Use support such as a chair as needed but the goal is to have
you practice the exercises given in the program to get you down to the floor safely
and back up again.

#5-SINGLE LEG TEST This test helps you identify whether you are at risk for falling.
Please make sure to have a chair in front of you during this test for safety. Perform
this test with your eyes open and arms on your hips.
You must stand unassisted on one leg and be timed in seconds from the time one
foot is flexed off the floor (not too high, just enough not to be touching the floor)
to the time when it touches the ground or the standing leg or an arm leaves the
hips.
