PERIMENOPAUSE
The lead-up to menopause -- usually starts in your late 30s to early
40s. Hormones begin to fluctuate erratically.
MENOPAUSE
Officially defined as 12 months after your last period. Average age is
51 -- but surgical menopause can happen much earlier.
WHY IT MATTERS FOR PD
This hormonal transition overlaps with the window when
Parkinson's symptoms may first emerge or worsen. It's a critical
time.

Muscle Is Your Superpower
Building Strength in Midlife with Parkinson's
After menopause, lean muscle mass declines
faster -- and for women with Parkinson's, this
compounds existing challenges with balance,
posture, and rigidity.
Muscle is an organ of longevity.
Strength training -- not cardio -- is the priority.
Cardio is good. Lifting is essential.
In the PPE program, we build both.
01 Strength train 2-3x/week
Boosts metabolism, preserves muscle, improves
PD motor function
02 Increase protein intake
Target 1.6g per kg body weight. Prioritize protein
at every meal.
03 Stay hydrated
Estrogen loss causes dryness. Aim for 2L of water
per day.
You're in the Driver's Seat
What to start doing today
Talk to Your Doctor
Share what you've learned today. Ask about
adjusting your PD medications around hormonal
changes. Advocate for yourself.
Lift Heavier Than You Think
Weight training is more important than cardio.
Start where you are and progress. Your bones,
muscles, and brain all benefit.
Explore HRT
Hormone Replacement Therapy may improve
symptoms -- discuss with your gynaecologist AND
neurologist together. One size does not fit all.
Track Your Symptoms
Note when symptoms change relative to hormonal
shifts. This information is gold for your healthcare
team.
Eat to Support Your Brain
Anti-inflammatory foods: berries, olive oil, fatty
fish. Limit sugar -- inflammation worsens both
menopause and PD. Protein first.
