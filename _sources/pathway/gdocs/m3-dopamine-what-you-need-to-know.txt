Dopamine & Parkinson’s
How to work with your brain — not against it  
What is dopamine?
Dopamine is a chemical messenger in the brain that helps with movement, motivation, focus, mood, and learning. In
Parkinson’s disease, dopamine-producing cells slowly decline — but dopamine is not gone. 
How you use what remains matters more than ever.
Think of dopamine like a bank account
You may have fewer funds, but you can still spend them wisely. Your goal is to protect dopamine, use it efficiently, and get more benefit from every drop.
How to make the most of your dopamine
■ Exercise regularly: Improves how efficiently the brain uses dopamine. Consistency beats intensity.
■ Challenge your brain: Dual■task activities (thinking + moving) strengthen brain pathways.
■■ Manage energy: Do important tasks when you feel sharp. Rest before exhaustion.
■■ Eat for brain health: Adequate protein, vegetables, fibre, and hydration support dopamine.
■ Protect sleep: Sleep resets brain chemistry and improves dopamine function.
■ Stay connected: Purpose, goals, and social connection support motivation and brain health.
The bottom line
Parkinson’s does not mean your brain is broken. It means it needs more intention, structure, and consistency. 
Small daily actions add up. Keep showing up — it matters.
