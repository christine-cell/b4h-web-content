You're Doing Better Than You Think
A message about anxiety, Parkinson's, and being human
Let's talk about something that doesn't get said out loud enough: anxiety and Parkinson's often
travel together and that doesn't mean you're weak, failing, or "not coping well enough."
I live with anxiety myself. I've had my own struggles, and I still do. This isn't something you just "fix" and
move on from. It's something you learn to understand, work with, and soften over time. Some days are
easier. Some days are heavier. That's human.
When you're living with Parkinson's, anxiety makes complete sense. Y our nervous system is already
working harder. Y our brain is constantly processing movement, balance, timing, and uncertainty. Add
fear of symptoms, fear of falling, fear of the future and it's no wonder anxiety shows up.
Here's the important part: anxiety is not a personal flaw. It's a nervous system response. It's your body
trying sometimes clumsily to protect you.
The Good News: Nervous Systems Can Be Trained
This is one of the reasons exercise especially the kind we do is so powerful. Not because it makes
Parkinson's disappear, but because it sends a powerful signal to your brain: "I am capable. I am
moving. I am safe enough in this moment."
Every time you show up, even on the hard days, you're doing more than exercising your body. Y ou're
teaching your nervous system that it doesn't have to stay stuck in fear.

What Progress Really Looks Like
Progress doesn't always look like feeling calm. Sometimes progress looks like showing up anyway.
Sometimes it looks like taking breaks. Sometimes it looks like giving yourself permission to have a
tough day without judgment.
Be gentle with yourself. I'm learning to do the same.
And remember this space is not about perfection. It's about support, consistency, and learning how to
live well with what you're carrying, not fighting yourself every step of the way.
