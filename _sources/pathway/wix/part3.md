Even one new habit – like adding a side salad most days – can make a meaningful difference over time.
**Simple ideas to get started**
• Add 1 extra serving of vegetables each day (for example, a small salad, carrot sticks, cooked greens or mixed frozen vegetables).
• Have berries (fresh or frozen) with breakfast a few times per week.
• Use olive oil instead of butter or margarine where possible.
• Aim for fish once a week, if you enjoy it and it fits your medical advice.
• Have beans, lentils or chickpeas in soups, stews, salads or dips once or twice a week.
• Swap one highly processed snack (chips, cookies) for a small handful of nuts or a piece of fruit.
• Drink more water and fewer sugary drinks. Try water with lemon, herbal tea or sparkling water.
This checklist is not a test and there is no “perfect” score. Parkinson’s looks different for everyone, and so does a supportive way of eating. Use this as a gentle guide, and always check with your health care team or dietitian before making big changes to your diet.

### Magnesium and Its Benefits for Parkinson’s Disease
#### Magnesium: The Essential Mineral
- Magnesium (Mg) is critical for life but cannot be made by the body; it must come from food or supplements.
- Daily Recommended Intake (RDI): Men: 420 mg; Women: 320 mg (most women are deficient in this)
#### Why Many People Are Deficient
- Around 50% of people in Western countries don’t get enough magnesium.
- Modern diets, with refined grains and depleted soil magnesium levels, contribute to this deficiency.
- In the Stone Age, people consumed about 4x more magnesium than we do today.
#### What Magnesium Does in the Body
- Supports energy production (ATP). - Regulates glucose, lipid, and protein metabolism. - Maintains nerve and muscle function, including stabilizing "edgy neurons." - Plays a role in reducing inflammation and oxidative stress. - Helps regulate calcium, which affects muscle contractions and nerve signaling.
#### Benefits of Magnesium for Parkinson’s Disease (PD)
1. **Neuroprotection**: Reduces neuronal excitability to protect brain cells. Lowers inflammation and oxidative stress, which damage neurons in PD.
2. **Dopamine Support**: Enhances dopamine production, the key neurotransmitter affected in PD. Supports enzymes needed for dopamine synthesis.
3. **Improves Medication Effectiveness**: Helps optimize L-DOPA therapy, the main treatment for PD.
4. **Calms Hyperactive Neurons**: Magnesium acts as a natural blocker of NMDA receptors to reduce excitotoxicity (nerve overactivity).
#### Signs of Magnesium Deficiency
- **Neurological**: Tremors, poor coordination, irritability, difficulty focusing. - **Muscular**: Cramps, spasms, twitching. - **Psychological**: Anxiety, depression. - **Sleep Issues**: Poor sleep quality, restless legs.
#### How to Check Magnesium Levels
- **Blood Tests**: Serum magnesium (basic), RBC magnesium (better reflection of long-term status). - **Hair Testing**: Can indicate long-term deficiencies in ADHD, ASD, and neurological issues.
#### Key Takeaway for Parkinson’s Patients
- Magnesium may help slow the progression of PD by protecting neurons, supporting dopamine production, and reducing inflammation.
- You can definitely work on increasing your magnesium intake through food but it's challenging to stay consistent enough or take enough to benefit from it.
#### Foods High in Magnesium — Nuts, seeds, leafy greens, whole grains, and legumes.
By focusing on magnesium, you can support better brain health and overall well-being as you manage Parkinson’s.

### Magnesium for Women: Supporting Hormonal Health as We Age
Magnesium plays an essential role in hormonal balance, making it particularly important for women as they age. Dr. Mary Claire Haver, an OB/GYN and expert in women’s health, emphasizes magnesium's impact on managing hormonal fluctuations, especially during menopause and perimenopause.
#### Key Benefits of Magnesium for Hormonal Health
1. **Reduces Menopause Symptoms**: Magnesium helps alleviate common symptoms such as hot flashes, night sweats, and mood swings by calming the nervous system and reducing inflammation.
2. **Supports Hormone Regulation**: Magnesium is essential for balancing estrogen and progesterone levels, which can fluctuate dramatically during perimenopause and menopause. It also supports adrenal health, helping the body adapt to stress, which impacts hormone production.
3. **Improves Sleep**: Magnesium enhances the production of melatonin, the hormone that regulates sleep, which often becomes disrupted during menopause. It calms the nervous system, making it easier to fall and stay asleep.
4. **Bone Health**: Estrogen decline in menopause increases the risk of osteoporosis. Magnesium works with calcium and vitamin D to maintain strong bones, reducing fracture risks.
5. **Eases PMS and Period Symptoms**: For women still menstruating, magnesium can reduce PMS symptoms such as bloating, cramps, and mood swings by relaxing muscles and regulating neurotransmitters.
6. **Mood and Stress**: Magnesium supports serotonin production (the "feel-good" hormone) and helps regulate cortisol levels, reducing anxiety and depression often associated with hormonal changes.
#### Why Women Are at Higher Risk of Deficiency
- Women often have increased magnesium needs due to pregnancy, breastfeeding, and hormonal shifts. - Menopause reduces magnesium absorption due to changes in gut health and metabolism.
#### Dr. Mary Claire Haver’s Recommendations
- Dr. Mary Claire advocates for daily magnesium intake through food and supplements to combat common issues like fatigue, brain fog, and sleep disturbances.
- Her preferred forms of magnesium include: **Magnesium glycinate**: Gentle on the stomach and great for stress and sleep. **Magnesium citrate**: Helps with constipation, common in aging women.
#### Best Foods for Women to Boost Magnesium
- Dark leafy greens (spinach, kale). - Nuts and seeds (almonds, pumpkin seeds). - Legumes (black beans, chickpeas). - Whole grains (quinoa, brown rice). - Dark chocolate (a delicious magnesium boost!).
#### Takeaway for Women
Magnesium is a critical mineral for hormonal health, sleep, stress management, and bone strength. As women age, ensuring adequate magnesium intake can significantly improve quality of life and alleviate the symptoms of aging and hormonal transitions.
